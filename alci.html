AQUAE LABS ECOSYSTEMS CONSERVATION INDEX (ALCI)

A Scientific Framework for Assessing Ecosystem Value

Assessing, quantifying and valuing ecosystem services through field-based ecological measurements and Monitoring, Recording and Verification (MRV).

Scientific Methodology

Moving Beyond Treating Nature Solely as a Conservation Cost

The AQUAE Labs Ecosystem Conservation Index (ALCI) is a scientific framework for assessing, quantifying and valuing ecosystem services through field-based ecological measurements and Monitoring, Recording and Verification (MRV).

ALCI evaluates key ecological functions and indicators, including biomass, oxygen production, water purification, cooling effects, soil properties and biodiversity. It aims to connect measured ecosystem conditions and performance with structured valuation methodologies and environmental asset development.

Rather than treating nature solely as a conservation cost, ALCI seeks to provide a framework through which ecological services can be assessed and their potential value communicated to institutions, project developers, investors and conservation stakeholders.

Living, Maturing Continuum

ALCI considers any ecosystem as a living, maturing continuum whose components vary in mass and value—consolidating and valuing measured outputs across seven core ecosystem-service domains.

DOMAIN 01 • OXYGEN

1. Gross Oxygen Productivity

Assesses the oxygen production associated with photosynthetic activity and ecosystem productivity.

In Simple Terms (Beginner Guide) Just like human lungs need fresh air, trees use sunlight to turn carbon dioxide into breathable oxygen. 1 hectare of mature analog forest produces ~435,511 liters of oxygen every year—ALCI measures and values this life-support output.

2. Water Purification Productivity

Evaluates the contribution of ecosystems to natural water purification processes.

What This Means Simply Forest canopies, deep roots, and soil microbes act like a giant natural water filter—cleaning up to 16.59 million liters of rainwater per hectare each year before it reaches rivers and wells.
Domain 03 • Carbon & Energy
Multi-Strata Biomass

3. Woody Biomass as Bulk Energy

Measures woody above-ground biomass as an indicator of ecosystem productivity and its potential as a source of renewable energy.

What This Means Simply As trees grow taller and thicker, their trunks and branches lock away massive amounts of carbon and solar energy—acting like a living green battery on the land.
Domain 04 • Microclimate
1.87M kWh / ha / yr

4. Cooling Effect Productivity

Assesses the contribution of vegetation and ecological succession to local temperature regulation and landscape cooling.

What This Means Simply Forests sweat cool water vapor (evapotranspiration) and shade the ground—acting as nature’s air conditioner with a cooling power equal to 1.87 million kWh per hectare annually.
Domain 05 • Pedogenesis
Mycorrhizal Web

5. Living Soil as a Sustainable Agriculture Product

Evaluates the ecological value of living soil, including its organic matter and role in supporting soil health and sustainable agriculture.

What This Means Simply Healthy soil isn’t dead dirt—it is a living underground city of beneficial fungi, earthworms, and organic nutrients that grows nutritious food without chemical fertilizers.
Domain 06 • WRC
Natural Sponge Matrix

6. Soil Water Retention Capacity (WRC)

Assesses the capacity of soil to retain water and support ecosystem functions and local hydrological processes.

What This Means Simply Organic-rich forest soil acts like a giant natural sponge—soaking up heavy monsoon rain to prevent flash floods and slowly releasing moisture during dry seasons.
Domain 07 • RSCV (Relative Species Conservation Value)

7. Biodiversity as a Conservation Product (RSCV)

Evaluates biodiversity through species richness and Relative Species Conservation Value (RSCV), recognising the conservation importance of species within an ecosystem.

What This Means Simply A plantation of one single tree species is fragile, whereas a biodiverse forest home to rare birds, amphibians, pollinators, and medicinal plants is resilient and irreplaceable. RSCV scores how many species—and how globally rare or endangered they are—thrive on the land.

Structured Methodology

How ALCI Works

ALCI follows a structured process to assess ecosystem conditions, measure ecological services and support the valuation of natural capital.

Step 01

1. Site Assessment and Land Profiling

The process begins with identifying and profiling the site, including its habitat type, ecological characteristics, land conditions and stages of ecological succession.

Simple Meaning Mapping the land’s boundaries, streams, and forest maturity zones (seral stages) using IUCN habitat standards.
Step 02

2. Field Data Collection

Ecological data are collected through field surveys, observations and measurements, covering biomass, ecosystem services, soil properties and biodiversity.

Simple Meaning Scientists and trained local monitors go into the field to measure real trees, soil moisture, water quality, and wildlife species.
Step 03

3. Monitoring, Recording and Verification (MRV)

Collected data are documented and assessed through the MRV framework to establish baseline conditions and monitor changes in ecosystem performance.

Simple Meaning Every field measurement is audited and re-verified on recurring 5-year cycles over a 33-year ecological timeline.
Step 04

4. Ecological Analysis and Valuation

Ecological indicators and defined calculation methods are used to assess ecosystem services and determine their associated values.

Simple Meaning Converting raw biological numbers (liters of oxygen, water purified, cooling kWh) into transparent natural capital indices.
Step 05

5. ALCI Assets Structuring

Assessed ecological values provide the basis for structuring ALCI Assets, subject to the applicable methodology and verification requirements.

Simple Meaning Packaging verified ecological outcomes into insured environmental assets where 45–55% of proceeds empower local land stewards.
Step 06

6. Digital Records and Traceability

AQUAEChain is designed to support digital recording, tracking and traceability of ecological data and associated environmental assets.

Simple Meaning Locking every verified ecological record onto the AQUAEChain digital ledger so no credit can ever be forged or double-counted.
Real-World ALCI Calibration Site

Ground-Truthed at Belipola Arboretum, Sri Lanka

Every ALCI equation is anchored in empirical field zoning. At the Belipola Arboretum (the world’s first Analog Forest research sanctuary in Mirahawatte, Sri Lanka), the landscape is partitioned into lettered ecological management sectors (Zones A through U), tracking river hydrology, cassia guilds, nursery propagation, and climax forest strata.

Methodological Credibility

From Ecological Evidence to Environmental Value

ALCI aims to establish a structured connection between the ecological functions of natural landscapes, their assessed value and the digital records associated with environmental assets. The credibility of this process depends on reliable field data, transparent methodologies, appropriate verification and clear communication of assessment limitations.
